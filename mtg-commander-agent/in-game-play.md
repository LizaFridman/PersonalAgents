# In-Game Play Heuristics

> Upload this file as Project knowledge, alongside the other curated docs. This is turn-by-turn play guidance — for when the player asks for tactical help mid-game, or reviews a game afterward. The deck-building counterpart is `deckbuilding-framework.md`; the rules counterpart is `commander-rules-reference.md`. Framework altitude: a checklist to keep advice consistent, not an exhaustive strategy text. Distilled from Reid Duke's "Level One" series (see `community-resources.md`), translated from 1v1 to multiplayer Commander — card advantage and board wipes matter *more* (you're outnumbered three-to-one; a wipe answers three boards with one card), tempo and play/draw matter *less*, there's no sideboard, and you hold a table role against each opponent separately. Offer this on request; don't turn every deck chat into a play lecture.

## Before the game — mulligans

- **Keep for function, not card count.** A keeper has the mana to operate its first few turns *and* does something toward the game plan without needing to draw a specific card. 2–4 lands is the usual band; adjust for the deck's curve.
- **Mulligan** all-land, all-spell, or all-in-on-one-undrawn-piece hands. Multiplayer Commander's first mulligan is free (then London mulligan with bottoming) — check `house-rules.md` for the table's variant — so use it rather than keeping a marginal seven.
- **Mulligan harder** when the deck (or a known opponent's deck) is fast: a hand that's fine against durdle tables is a mull against a turn-3 aggressor.

## During the game

- **Reassess your role every few turns.** "Who's the beatdown?" is answered separately against each opponent and shifts as players die. Press the attack when you're the aggressor; stop racing and trade/block when you're not. A fast draw can make a grindy deck the aggressor, and a slow draw the reverse.
- **Sequence for information.** Think through the whole turn before acting. Do info-gathering (draw, scry) and opponent-forcing plays (attacks, discard) early; do hand-revealing or self-committing plays (threats, combat tricks) late. Drop lands before combat to keep your mana ambiguous.
- **Cast at the last responsible moment — but don't play scared.** Hold reactive spells for the target that actually matters; deploy proactively into open mana when the relevant opponent is tapped out. Letting a counter or trick that may not exist freeze you out of advancing your plan is its own loss.
- **Don't overcommit into an open board.** Against untapped white/black mana or a known control deck, develop enough to apply pressure but keep a threat in reserve to rebuild through the wrath.
- **Play ahead vs. behind differently.** Ahead: simplify, trade freely down the table, close comeback routes, take your time on the lethal turn. Behind: keep resources and board complexity, accept the damage, play to the sequence that actually wins. In multiplayer you're often ahead of one player and behind another — act per opponent.
- **Count the clock, per opponent.** Racing is many-sided and table damage is additive against any one player. Evasion and reach beat a whole table of blockers; lifegain against one attacker while two others develop rarely changes the race.
- **Turn the corner.** Grindy decks lose by durdling. Once the engine is online and you're ahead, switch from developing to ending the game.

## After the game — diagnosing a loss

When reviewing a loss for the game log (`game-log-workflow.md`), name the strategic cause where there is one: misassigned role (raced when you should have blocked, or vice versa), no inevitability (durdled into a better long-game deck), overcommitted into a known wrath, all-answers-no-plan (never advanced a proactive threat), color-screwed despite an adequate pip ratio. These accumulate into diagnosable patterns on the deck's `wiki/<topic>.md` page instead of a bare win/loss record.
